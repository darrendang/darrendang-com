## Canonical interpretation
The 2026 letter is not primarily a memorial or eulogy. It is a 25-year accountability loop: the son who made a promise in 2001 returns in 2026 to ask what he did with what his father gave him.

The evaluative frame is based on Đặng Văn Phi's lived standards rather than conventional achievement: care for family; education; service beyond immediate family; perseverance through loss; generosity despite scarcity; and passing forward what one has learned.

The answer remains intentionally open and humble: **I think I have tried to. Not perfectly. Not completely. And not finished.**

A central lineage insight is also canonical: the father's late-night photocopying of school textbooks and Darren's present work of preserving and transmitting knowledge are not the same act, but they share an underlying principle—if something learned may help those who come after, do not keep it to yourself; pass it forward.

The line **“You never called it The Way. You just lived your way.”** is canonical for this artifact and clarifies provenance: The Way is a later articulation of patterns observed in lived experience; it must not be retroactively attributed to Đặng Văn Phi as his named framework.

## Evidence and provenance guardrails
- Preserve the 2001 letter as a historical primary source; do not silently modernize or rewrite it.
- Later family corrections or clarifications may sit beside the source but must not overwrite it.
- The song is an artistic memorial, not an authoritative historical record.
- Do not state or imply that photocopying caused Đặng Văn Phi's cancer; the family may wonder about the connection, but causation is not established.
- `Nghĩa` should remain culturally textured rather than flattened into a single English word; in this context it carries duty, love, sacrifice, honor, and responsibility arising from relationship.
